\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{chap:concl}

\section{Summary of Contributions}
This dissertation has presented a three-stage framework for developing wireless foundation models, focusing on self-supervised representation learning, large language model (LLM)-based reasoning, and meta-learning for rapid adaptation. Our work makes the following primary contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{DenoMAE and CDRF: Self-Supervised, Vision-Based Representation Learning (Chapters~\ref{chap:denomae}--\ref{chap:cdrf})}
    DenoMAE is a self-supervised learning framework, co-developed with Atik Faysal (lead), that learns robust and transferable representations from unlabeled wireless signals by treating noise as an explicit modality and jointly processing time-domain IQ samples and constellation diagrams. The CageDroneRF (CDRF) benchmark complements it with large-scale, real-world over-the-air evidence (39 classes, 500+ GB of raw I/Q) that vision architectures---YOLO for detection, ResNet for hierarchical and open-set classification---transfer effectively to RF perception. Together these works address RQ1, establishing that image-based self-supervision gives label-efficient RF features (82.4\% on a shared 10-class benchmark; 0.986 mAP@0.5 on real drone spectrograms), with clear bounds: performance collapses under extreme noise ($-30$ dB) and transfers weakly, without adaptation, to an unseen dataset (RadioML).

    \item \textbf{Plug-and-Play and DiSC-AMC: LLM-Based Reasoning for Open-Set AMC (Chapter~\ref{chap:plugandplay})}
    Plug-and-Play first demonstrated that a pre-trained LLM, given statistical signal summaries as text, can perform open-set modulation classification without wireless-specific training. \textbf{DiSC-AMC}~\cite{disc_amc}, this dissertation's central contribution to RQ2, resolves Plug-and-Play's token-efficiency bottleneck through feature discretization, self-supervised context retrieval, and candidate-space pruning: a QLoRA fine-tuned 7B model reaches 83.0\% in-distribution and 82.5\% out-of-distribution accuracy on a synthetic 10-class benchmark, surpassing all supervised baselines, while a training-free 7B configuration reaches 71.9\% accuracy at 0.5K tokens---exceeding a 200B-parameter baseline given 2.9K tokens of raw statistics. On cross-dataset transfer to RadioML, DiSC-AMC amplifies a frozen or fine-tuned encoder's own classification head by up to 8$\times$. Together, Plug-and-Play and DiSC-AMC provide the dissertation's affirmative answer to RQ2, bounded by the quality of the encoder whose embeddings the pipeline retrieves over.

    \item \textbf{Meta-Task: Rapid Adaptation through Meta-Learning (Chapter~\ref{chap:meta_task})}
    We introduced Meta-Task, a method-agnostic approach to learnable regularization in few-shot learning, addressing RQ3. On vision few-shot benchmarks, Meta-Task delivers consistent, if uneven, gains (e.g., 65.4\%$\to$71.3\% for Prototypical Networks on Tiered-ImageNet 5-way 5-shot, converging within 20k episodes versus more than 50k without it). Tested directly as a wireless deployment through the DiSC-AMC pipeline on RadioML, however, Meta-Task gives \emph{no net gain} over plain fine-tuning (22.2\% vs.\ 23.0\%)---a robust null result confirmed under two independent protocol fixes. RQ3's answer is therefore \emph{partly} affirmative: the mechanism helps where the base learner's inductive bias is weak and the domain is in-distribution, but is not a substitute for a better representation under cross-dataset wireless transfer.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Key Findings}
Each research question is answered following the same structure: hypothesis, controlled evidence, a difficult finding, and a bounded answer.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Answer to RQ1 (Represent)---yes, within bounds:} Image-based self-supervision (DenoMAE, Chapter~\ref{chap:denomae}) gives label-efficient RF features---82.4\% on a shared 10-class synthetic benchmark (+22.1 points over no pretraining at $-10$ dB) and 0.986 mAP@0.5 for real drone detection (CDRF, Chapter~\ref{chap:cdrf}). The bound is explicit: representations collapse to near-chance below roughly $-30$ dB, and features trained on synthetic constellations transfer weakly, without adaptation, to an unseen dataset (RadioML).
    \item \textbf{Answer to RQ2 (Reason)---yes, with compact symbols and retrieved examples:} DiSC-AMC (Chapter~\ref{chap:plugandplay}) shows that a 7B LLM performs open-set AMC competitively with or better than dedicated supervised encoders (83.0\% in-distribution, 82.5\% out-of-distribution) when fine-tuned, and remains competitive training-free (71.9\% at 0.5K tokens, versus 69.92\% for a 200B model at 2.9K tokens). The bound: accuracy is only as good as the retrieval embedding it reasons over, and also collapses near $-30$ dB.
    \item \textbf{Answer to RQ3 (Adapt)---partly:} Meta-Task's learnable regularization (Chapter~\ref{chap:meta_task}) helps few-shot vision benchmarks (e.g., +5.9 points for Prototypical Networks on Tiered-ImageNet) but gives no reliable gain on cross-dataset wireless AMC through the DiSC-AMC pipeline (22.2\% vs.\ 23.0\%, a robust null result). The lesson: on wireless transfer, the representation is the bottleneck, not the regularizer---directly connecting RQ3 back to RQ1.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Composition.} The three answers are not independent: DiSC-AMC (RQ2) uses an RQ1 encoder's embeddings for retrieval inside its LLM reasoner, lifting RadioML accuracy up to 8$\times$ over the encoder alone---a concrete demonstration that the framework's components compose rather than merely coexist. The RQ3 wireless test, in turn, points back to RQ1: on a new dataset, representation quality limits accuracy more than adaptation strategy does.

\section{Limitations}
Three limitations bound every result in this dissertation, each surfaced directly by the experiments rather than assumed in advance:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Extreme noise:} Image-based representations (DenoMAE, CDRF) and the retrieval they enable (DiSC-AMC) collapse to near-random accuracy at approximately $-30$ dB SNR, where constellation diagrams become effectively featureless. This is a shared failure mode across every vision-rooted component of the framework, not an isolated weakness of one method.
    \item \textbf{Dataset transfer:} Encoders trained on synthetic AMC data (DenoMAE2.0, DINOv2) transfer weakly to the real-world RadioML benchmark without target-domain adaptation; DiSC-AMC's reasoning amplifies a weak or unadapted encoder but cannot substitute for a representation whose nearest neighbors are uninformative on the new dataset.
    \item \textbf{Wireless adaptation:} Meta-Task gives no net gain on RadioML AMC (22.2\% vs.\ 23.0\%); its demonstrated gains are confined to in-distribution, vision few-shot benchmarks. Rapid, few-shot wireless adaptation therefore remains an open problem for future work, not a solved component of the framework.
    \item \textbf{Scale and scope of validation:} Experiments are conducted primarily on 10- and 24-class synthetic/RadioML benchmarks plus one real-world drone RF benchmark (CDRF); broader modulation taxonomies, multi-antenna/MIMO scenarios, and hardware-in-the-loop deployment remain untested.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Research Directions}
\label{sec:future_work}

The framework validated in this dissertation leaves several directions open, some already probed with a first (negative) result rather than left purely speculative.

\subsection{A First Fusion Attempt, and Why It Did Not Help}

The most natural integration test---layering Meta-Task's learnable regularization on top of the DiSC-AMC pipeline on RadioML---was already run (Section~\ref{sec:wireless_meta_task}) and gave no net gain (22.2\% vs.\ 23.0\% for plain fine-tuning). This is a useful negative result, not an unexplored opportunity: it shows that naively stacking the framework's adaptation module onto its reasoning module does not help when the underlying representation is the bottleneck. A more promising fusion direction, left untested here, is feeding Meta-Task-adapted \emph{encoder} features (rather than a frozen or vanilla fine-tuned DenoMAE2.0/DINOv2 backbone) into DiSC-AMC's retrieval stage, so that adaptation improves the exemplars being retrieved rather than only regularizing a classification head that DiSC-AMC's reasoning already bypasses.

\subsection{Vision-Language Models for Richer Reasoning}

DiSC-AMC currently reasons over discretized \emph{statistics} of the constellation diagram, discarding the image itself after the self-supervised encoder reduces it to an embedding. A vision-language model (VLM) that reasons jointly over the constellation image and the discretized context could recover information the current pipeline discards---for instance, visual symmetry or point-cloud density patterns that are only coarsely captured by moment- and cumulant-based statistics. This is a natural next step for RQ2 that keeps the framework's modularity: the VLM would replace only the LLM reasoning stage, not the retrieval or discretization stages.

\subsection{Open Gaps Identified During Evaluation}

Several specific, scoped gaps were identified while running the DiSC-AMC and Meta-Task evaluations and are left as concrete follow-up work rather than folded into the main results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Shots-per-class sweep:} The sensitivity of DiSC-AMC and Plug-and-Play to the \emph{number} of in-context examples per class (1 / 2 / 4 shots) was not swept; only the choice of \emph{which} examples to retrieve (FAISS vs.\ centroid) was ablated (Section~\ref{sec:disc_ablations}).
    \item \textbf{A fair non-generative-model test:} Preliminary tests substituting a non-generative scoring model (``Jev'') for the DiSC-AMC prompt surfaced several confounds---a prompt not tailored for non-generative decoding, criteria that cannot carry query-specific evidence, and a bug that sent null option descriptions in default runs. A fair comparison requires query-specific criteria and repeated runs at larger sample sizes than the 200--240 queries tested here.
    \item \textbf{Explicit SNR-aware prompting:} Performance degrades gracefully but monotonically with SNR across every LLM-based configuration; none of the current prompts explicitly condition on an SNR estimate, which could let the LLM calibrate its own confidence rather than reasoning as if every query were equally reliable.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Additional Promising Directions}

Beyond these, several secondary avenues warrant exploration:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scaling to Broader Modulation Taxonomies:} Extending pretraining and evaluation to 50+ modulation schemes, including emerging waveforms (OTFS, NOMA) and legacy standards (analog FM/AM), to build truly general-purpose encoders and reasoning pipelines.

    \item \textbf{Multi-Task Foundation Models:} Training or prompting unified pipelines that perform modulation classification, SNR estimation, channel equalization, and interference detection simultaneously, leveraging shared representations across tasks.

    \item \textbf{Real-Time Edge Deployment:} Quantifying the latency and memory footprint of the training-free DiSC-AMC configuration (5B--7B models, 0.5--1.3K-token prompts) on embedded GPU or FPGA targets, where the token-efficiency gains demonstrated here would translate directly into deployment feasibility.

    \item \textbf{Cross-Domain Transfer:} Evaluating whether the representations and reasoning pipelines developed for AMC and drone detection in this dissertation transfer to radar, satellite, or biomedical RF applications, testing the generality claimed by the foundation-model framing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Closing Remarks}
This dissertation has laid the groundwork for a new generation of wireless AI systems based on the principles of foundation models. By integrating self-supervised learning, LLM-based reasoning, and meta-learning, we have demonstrated a path toward building general-purpose, adaptable, and interpretable models for the wireless domain. The vision is to move beyond specialized, black-box classifiers and toward a future of truly cognitive radios that can understand, reason about, and adapt to the complexities of the wireless world.
